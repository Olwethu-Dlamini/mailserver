Improving the System as Built

It works: mail arrives from Gmail through Cloudflare and the Worker, and leaves through Resend with DMARC passing at a strict receiver. Three days of logs say what to fix next. This page lists each problem, the evidence for it, and the change I would make, ordered by how much it matters.

Revised 12 October 2026 after comparing with what others run (part 12): the ARC proposal was withdrawn and the backup method corrected. Measured on 11 October 2026 from srv1 (Stalwart 0.16.25) and public DNS. The counts cover the Stalwart logs for 8 to 11 October. A change that has not been made yet is marked as a proposal.

1. Real mail is going to Junk

Between 8 and 11 October Stalwart delivered 9 inbound messages into mailboxes. 3 were filed as ham and 6 as message-ingest.spam. One of the 6 was my own unsigned loopback test from example.org, which deserved it. The other 5 were transactional mail from Anthropic, Claude and HubSpot. The 11 October one from email.claude.com logged smtp.dkim-pass and smtp.dmarc-pass and still went to Junk.

The likely cause is the shape of the inbound path, not the senders. Every message reaches Stalwart from 127.0.0.1, so SPF fails for every sender (part 09 already lists smtp.spf-from-fail as expected), and the spam filter cannot score the real sending IP. A soft SPF fail plus a bulk-mail sender is enough to tip the score. I have not read the score breakdown yet, which is the first step:

  1. In Roundcube, open one of the junked messages, then More › View source. Stalwart's X-Spam-Status and X-Spam-Result headers name each rule that fired and its weight. That turns "likely" into a fact.
  2. Proposal: stop checking SPF for tunnel sessions. SPF on a connection from 127.0.0.1 can only ever fail, so it adds noise and no information. Stalwart's maintainer gave the recipe for exactly this case on the support forum in June 2026: give the relay its own listener, and in the sender-authentication expressions set the SPF EHLO and SPF MAIL FROM checks to disable when listener (or remote_ip) is the tunnel's. DKIM and DMARC still verify, because the Worker passes the message bytes through unchanged, and they are what decide in practice already.
  3. Not an option: ARC. The first version of this page proposed trusting Cloudflare's ARC seal to carry its SPF verdict through. Cloudflare's own issue tracker says otherwise: workerd #6740 (open since May 2026) shows that mail reaching an Email Worker carries arc=none, no Authentication-Results and no Received-SPF. The real sending IP never reaches the Worker, so nothing downstream can check SPF. What does arrive is an x-cf-spamh-score header, which a Sieve rule or a custom spam-filter rule can use as an extra signal.
  4. Train the filter. Moving a message from Junk to Inbox teaches Stalwart's Bayes classifier. Do it for the 5 now, and check that spam training is switched on under the spam filter settings.
Why this comes first

A mail server that delivers to Junk feels like one that loses mail, because nobody checks Junk. Password resets and receipts are exactly the mail this domain is for, and that is the mail being misfiled.

The same address, a bigger risk: auto-ban

Stalwart bans IPs automatically: by default, 35 abuse events a day (relay attempts, probing for unknown recipients) or 100 failed logins, and with no ban period set the ban is permanent (auto-ban docs). Every inbound message arrives from the one tunnel address, so a busy day of spam to invented addresses at the catch-all could ban 127.0.0.1 and refuse all inbound mail. Check the blocked-IP list in the admin UI, set a ban period, and make sure the tunnel's listener or address is exempt.

2. A fallback when home is down

The Worker (worker/src/index.ts) has two outcomes. A permanent SMTP error from Stalwart becomes message.setReject(), which bounces to the sender. Any other failure (tunnel down, srv1 rebooting, a power cut at home) is re-thrown, and the message's fate is then up to Email Routing and the sender's retry logic. There is no second place for the mail to go.

Proposal: on a temporary failure, forward a copy to a backup mailbox before giving up:

} catch (error) {
  if (error instanceof SmtpPermanentError) {
    message.setReject(error.message);
    return;
  }
  // srv1 or the tunnel is unreachable: keep the mail somewhere rather than nowhere
  await message.forward(env.FALLBACK_ADDRESS);
}

FALLBACK_ADDRESS is a new variable in wrangler.jsonc, and the address has to be added and verified under Email Routing › Destination addresses first. A Gmail address works. The cost: during an outage, mail sits in Gmail instead of Stalwart, and I move it back by hand. The benefit: no outage at home loses mail.

3. DMARC reports are spending the send quota

Stalwart sends DMARC aggregate reports back to each domain that sent me mail. The logs show 6 reports queued in three days, to mailauth-reports@google.com, dmarc-reports@anthropic.com and Cloudflare's report address. Another 3 were refused as outgoing-report.unauthorized-reporting-address and 3 as dmarc-rate-limited. Every report that goes out uses the resend route and counts against the free plan's 100 a day and 3,000 a month. The more mail I receive, the more reports I send.

Proposal: turn outgoing DMARC (and TLS) aggregate reports off under Stalwart's reporting settings. Nobody depends on a personal server's reports. Receiving reports is a separate setting and should stay on: the _dmarc record's rua=mailto:postmaster@22112002.xyz and the _smtp._tls TLS-RPT record are how I learn who is sending as me. Check that postmaster@ exists as an alias on my account, or those reports bounce.

4. Backups that leave the building

A nightly timer, stalwart-backup.timer, runs at 03:30 and writes two files to /var/backups/stalwart: a tarball of the Stalwart config, env file, RocksDB directory and the Roundcube config, and a mariadb-dump of the Roundcube database. It keeps 14 days, and every run since 8 October finished. Three things are missing:

  • The copy is on the same virtual disk. If srv1's disk or the Proxmox host dies, the backup dies with the original. Copy the newest pair off the VM each night: to pve or the laptop over Tailscale with rsync, or encrypted with restic to a free Cloudflare R2 bucket (10 GB free; the archive is 3 MB today).
  • RocksDB is copied while Stalwart runs. The script's own comment says so. The supported recipe, from Stalwart's support forum (thread #386, May 2026), is to stop the service, copy the whole /var/lib/stalwart directory, and start it again: a few seconds of downtime at 03:30. Stalwart's --export also needs the server stopped, and its docs say it is "not a substitute for proper backup procedures", so it is not the answer either.
  • No restore has been tested. Once: start a scratch VM, unpack last night's archive, start Stalwart, log in with Roundcube. A backup is only real once it has been restored.

5. Find out before a sender does

Part 09's mailcheck.sh is good at finding a fault once I suspect one. Nothing tells me there is one. The cheapest end-to-end alarm tests the whole loop on a schedule:

  1. A timer on srv1 (or the laptop) sends a message once a day from olwethu@22112002.xyz to a Gmail address, through Stalwart, so through Resend.
  2. A Gmail filter auto-forwards anything with that subject back to a second address, such as canary@22112002.xyz, so through Email Routing, the Worker and the tunnel.
  3. The timer checks the canary mailbox over IMAP an hour later. If the message is there, it pings a free healthchecks.io check; if the ping does not arrive, healthchecks.io emails or messages me.

That one loop covers DNS, both Cloudflare hops, the tunnel, Stalwart, Resend and Gmail's acceptance. Two cheaper extras: a systemd OnFailure= hook on stalwart and cloudflared, and Resend's webhook for email.bounced pointed at the same alert channel.

6. Narrow the listeners

ss -ltnp on 11 October shows Stalwart bound to * on 25, 465, 993, 995, 4190, 443 and 8080. srv1 has three addresses: loopback, the LAN (10.0.0.10) and Tailscale. The only nftables rules are Tailscale's, and the INPUT policy is accept. So anything on the home LAN can reach the admin UI over plain HTTP on 8080, and can talk SMTP to port 25 directly, without Cloudflare's filtering in front.

Proposal:

  • Bind 25 to 127.0.0.1. The Worker arrives through cloudflared, which connects to loopback, so nothing changes for it.
  • Bind 8080 (admin) to 127.0.0.1 and the Tailscale address only. Remote admin goes over Tailscale, as it did during the build.
  • Drop the POP3 listener (995) if no client uses it. Keep 4190 only if Sieve filters will be managed from Roundcube (part 11).
  • Or, more simply, add a small nftables table that accepts loopback and tailscale0, plus 80 from the LAN for Caddy, and drops the rest.

Glossary for this part

False positive (spam)
Legitimate mail the spam filter scored as spam. The failure that matters most for a personal mailbox, because Junk is rarely read.
ARC
Authenticated Received Chain. Headers a forwarding hop adds to record the SPF, DKIM and DMARC results it saw, signed so that the next hop can trust them. Email Routing does not provide useful ones to Workers (arc=none), so it cannot rescue SPF on this design.
Auto-ban
Stalwart's automatic IP blocking after too many abuse events or failed logins. Dangerous when every inbound connection shares one address.
Fallback forward
The Worker sending a copy to a verified backup address when it cannot reach Stalwart, so an outage at home delays mail instead of losing it.
Canary message
A scheduled test message that goes all the way round, out and back in, so that a silent failure anywhere on the path becomes a missed check-in.
Outgoing DMARC report
An aggregate report my server sends to other domains about the mail they sent me. Optional, and on this design it is paid for out of the Resend quota.
